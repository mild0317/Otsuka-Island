# Real star catalogue (v88)

`star-catalog-v88.js` contains 41,411 stars down to apparent magnitude 8, with HIP identifiers, J2000 right ascension/declination in degrees, visual magnitude, B−V colour index, and selected bright-star names. It also contains 89 constellation figures (Serpens is split into two figures).

Data were packed from Olaf Frohn's [d3-celestial](https://github.com/ofrohn/d3-celestial) `stars.8.json`, `starnames.json`, `constellations.json`, and `constellations.lines.json`, retrieved 2026-10-28. The original GeoJSON structure was replaced with compact arrays; numerical star coordinates and magnitudes were preserved. The project's BSD-3-Clause notice is retained in [star-catalog-LICENSE.txt](star-catalog-LICENSE.txt).

The stellar data source credited by d3-celestial is **Anderson E., Francis C. (2012), XHIP: An Extended Hipparcos Compilation**, [CDS V/137D](https://cdsarc.cds.unistra.fr/viz-bin/ReadMe/V/137D?format=html). Constellation line figures are illustrative conventions, not IAU constellation boundaries. Names and figure positions follow d3-celestial; common bright-star Chinese labels are provided by this app.

The local star-map engine uses Taipei (25.033°N, 121.5654°E), UTC+8 scene dates, J2000-to-date IAU 1976 precession, mean sidereal rotation, and equatorial-to-horizontal coordinates. Formula references:

- [USNO: Computing Approximate Sidereal Time](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/faq/GAST)
- [USNO: Computing Altitude and Azimuth](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/faq/alt_az)
- [University of Tokyo S2E: Celestial Rotation / precession](https://www.space.t.u-tokyo.ac.jp/s2e-documents/Specifications/Environment/Spec_CelestialRotation.html)

This is an approximate visual star map, not a precision ephemeris: UTC approximates UT1; stellar proper motion, nutation, atmospheric refraction, aberration, planets, and light pollution are omitted. V98 uses a local angular sky window centred on the island observer's southeast direction (azimuth 150 degrees), elevation 45 degrees. Horizontal sky longitude is atan2(right, forward); vertical sky latitude is atan2(top, hypot(right, forward)). Unlike the V95-V97 orthographic disc, this fills a rectangular viewport without revealing a circular hemisphere rim when zoomed out. V99 reduces the angular scale from 0.455 to 0.33 times the longer viewport dimension (about 27.5 percent smaller than V98). The front-hemisphere and latitude limits stay outside the longest viewport edges. Constellation guides use thinner lines and smaller labels, and fade with the same night strength as stars. This represents a view from inside a large world under a distant sky, rather than a finite star globe surrounding the island. Catalogue stars, constellation arcs and galactic dust share the same sidereal rotation, precession and projection. Broad tracks may have shallow segments or inflections; they are not a rigid horizontal translation. No perspective depth divisor is used. Physical above-horizon visibility is still evaluated at the requested observer. The date is automatic; the scene time multiplier can advance it naturally, and v91 displays the playback date as read-only. Real star positions do not depend on the session's random seed. Magnitude-based bloom, scintillation, meteors, and unresolved Milky Way dust are artistic effects. The Milky Way dust shares the celestial rotation and the J2000 galactic plane orientation (north pole RA 192.85948 degrees, Dec 27.12825 degrees; centre RA 266.4051 degrees, Dec -28.936175 degrees). Since v91 the displayed sun and moon follow independent left-to-right scene paths, rising at the left cloud horizon and setting at the right; those display positions are not astronomical coordinates. Lunar phase still uses local [Astronomy Engine](vendor/astronomy-sources.md) calculations.
